Handling HTTP 429

How a client should respond to throttling. Most prolonged throttling we investigate is made worse by the client's own retries.

Honour Retry-After

When a request is refused with HTTP 429, wait at least the number of seconds in Retry-After before sending the next request with that client ID, not only before retrying the refused one. Pause the whole sender, not each message separately.

Back off with jitter

If no Retry-After is given, retry with exponential back-off and full jitter: start at 1 second, double each attempt, cap at 60 seconds, and choose a random wait between zero and that value. Limit retries to 5 a message, then hold the message in your own queue rather than drop it.

Pace your sends

Send at no more than 90% of your client's limit, with a token bucket or similar rate limiter in front of the API. A paced sender is never throttled in normal operation and drains a backlog at a steady rate.

Use the batch endpoint

POST /v2/messages:batch accepts up to 100 messages in one request, and a batch counts as one request against the limit. Moving high-volume notifications to batches is the most effective way to stay under a limit without raising it.

Do not scale out to clear throttling

Adding instances that send with the same client ID does not add capacity: they share one limit, so more instances means more refused requests. Scale out only after the client's limit has been raised, or give each workload its own client ID.
